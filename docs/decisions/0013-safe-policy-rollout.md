## Decision

1. **Roll out on a fixed stage ladder — shadow → 1% → 5% → 25% → 50% → 100% — where every stage transition is a share vector over `policy_id`s and nothing else.** Shadow admits on: ≥ 24 h of fleet traffic AND ≥ 50,000 shadow decisions AND replay PASS AND the ADR-0009 invariants (`dbl = 0`, `I1 = 0` read on the executed side). A cold shadow — nothing to inherit — is an R102 violation and blocks the rollout; it is a defect to fix, not a stage to wait out. Stage dwell is sized in **decisions, not wall clock** (the wall clock is the deployment's arrival rate).
2. **The canary gate is a SET of independent members; abort (auto-rollback) fires on exactly two of them.** (a) *Margin*: in matched windows of W=1,000 decisions per side (equal-count; both sides must clear the count), D = margin ¢/1k(canary) − margin ¢/1k(control) on routed decisions; abort on **2 consecutive gate-windows (gw=4 raw windows) with D < −Y, or one with D < −1.5Y**. (b) *Routability*: canary-vs-control unroutable-share delta > 5 pts in 2 consecutive windows → abort. **Y = 3σ of the live null, re-measured continuously** — never a constant in a config. The remaining members *block promotion, they do not abort*: coverage (arms touched at matched decision counts, cumulative across stages, vs the incumbent's own curve), replay PASS, `dbl=0`/`I1=0`, and the stage D-trace promotion check (stage mean D ≥ −2·s.e., σ from the pooled null).
3. **Warm start is fold inheritance, not a prior.** A candidate boots from the incumbent's newest snapshot and replays the same WAL (one WAL, many folds). A partial warm start — per-processor aggregate rates as a prior artifact at m=100 — is valid *as an artifact* (a deployable `policy_id`, bounded regret by ADR-0006's design, measured riding the gate at −390 c/1k) and is **not** a substitute for the fold: a cold fold costs 324 c/1k in its first 10k transactions on a deploy with nothing else wrong [S1]. Prior-artifact refresh is itself a deploy-shaped event and enters this protocol through the same gate.
4. **Policy identity is content; versions are labels.** `policy_id = sha256` over the canonical tuple {algorithm, arm schema + band edges, prior artifact digest, policy seed, score/protocol config, catalog hash, ConstraintSet hash}. Equal content is the same policy no matter how many times it is deployed; any component change is a *different* policy that enters the ladder at shadow. Semver is a human-facing label on the same tuple, never the identity. Every DECISION_LOG row is trace-tagged with `policy_id` (the v1 run header hashes, plus the boot row's hashes as fleet-proof), which is what lets #15 separate versions.
5. **The kill-switch is one config change.** Rollback = atomic pointer swap to the previous `policy_id`'s share vector, sub-second propagation target; no fold rebuild, no data migration — the incumbent fold never stopped folding. The drill (measured propagation, lease handling, dashboard state) is #17's gate.

## Context

Three facts about this repo's shape decide the design:

- **The ticket's premise is mostly already fixed, and the residue is specific.** A new policy does not start with a cold posterior in this architecture: the fold over the WAL is policy-independent learned state (ADR-0006 §7, ADR-0012), so a candidate is warm at boot. What can still hurt is (i) a migration that loses the store — priced at [S1] — (ii) a candidate that is *wrong* in a way warm state does not cure — five regressions priced at [S3] — and (iii) the interaction of a rollout with the drift machinery — priced at [S4]. The protocol is sized for those three, not for a cold-start fear the architecture already answered.
- **The gate reads executed money, so it needs exposure, so it needs a measured null.** Shadow cannot clear margin (its decisions are never dispatched — its "margin" is a counterfactual only #15 can estimate, at plug-in grade). The canary is the first place real money moves, which makes the gate's false-abort rate a first-class quantity: it is measured here on the committed scenario's null (no-op deploy, seed rotation only, two assignment seeds pooled), not asserted.
- **Everything the gate needs already exists as accepted structure.** Key-addressed determinism (ADR-0005) makes the split a pure function; the snapshot + WAL (ADR-0012) makes warm start free and rollback a pointer swap; DECISION_LOG v1's header hashes (ADR-0008) already carry the version hook; ADR-0011 fixed the one suppression seam. This ADR adds the protocol and the gate, and closes the three payloads the prior ADRs deferred.

### What was measured (spike 0014, five regressions through the gate)

The spike implements the ladder end to end on the ADR-0005 harness — pure key-addressed assignment, a candidate fold beside the incumbent's, the suppression seam live — and runs: the identity checks [S0]; the cutover cost [S1]; shadow's gates and duration [S2]; the null and five regressions (excluded processor, ADR-0006's rejected sampler, miscalibrated prior ×1.25, λ_to mispricings 45→10 and 45→0) through the gate [S3]; the drift-detector interaction including a real outage mid-rollout [S4]; and the full ladder on `quiet-improvement-starved-v1` — the rollout the ticket fears, in the direction it hopes for [S5].

## Consideration-by-consideration analysis

### 1. The rollout protocol: shadow duration, the canary ramp, the abort criterion

**Shadow: how long, and what for.** Shadow = the candidate folding the **shared WAL** and logging decisions it does not execute. It is unbiased for free: it sees every settled outcome the incumbent's attempts produce (per-arm world facts, missing-at-random conditional on the incumbent — ADR-0008 C1), so the fold stays warm and the candidate's own estimates are live. What shadow **can** clear: decision-rule validity at scale (chains non-empty, constraint verdicts sane, propensities logged), replay bit-exactness (a shadow decision is the same pure function of fold + key-addressed draws), the deployment plumbing end to end, and free warm-up. What it **cannot** clear: executed margin — zero revenue risk buys zero revenue evidence [S2]. Measured: a warm-started shadow is at its decision-agreement plateau from the first window (75.9% over 3,846 attempt-0 decisions); a cold one claws up 59.9 → ~73% over its first 20k transactions (final 70.7% over 4,616 rows, fold MAE back to 4.25 pts) — the fold warms it, exactly as ADR-0006's cold-start table predicts, which is the proof that the wait is *validation*, not learning. **Admission: ≥ 24 h of fleet traffic AND ≥ 50k decisions AND replay PASS AND ADR-0009's gates.** Cost while shadowing: 13.0 GB/day at 5,000 dps (+29% on the 104 B DECISION_LOG row; 30 B/decision shadow row, labelled model per ADR-0012's byte discipline) and one extra `Decide()` on the sampled path; the fold is 135 KiB.

**The canary ramp.** 1% → 5% → 25% → 50% → 100%, dwell sized in decisions. The ladder's lower rungs are *health checks, not statistics instruments*: at 1% the candidate sees 100 decisions per 10k transactions — enough to catch crash-level brokenness (empty chains, constraint violations, replay mismatch), not enough to price anything. The pricing starts at 25%, where a W=1,000 window is 0.8 s of wall clock at fleet pace. Promotion between rungs requires every gate member clear; the coverage member is checked **cumulatively across stages** against the incumbent's own curve (the single-stage check is provably blind — see the rejected-sampler row below).

**The abort criterion.** D = margin ¢/1k(canary) − margin ¢/1k(control) over **matched equal-count windows** (1,000 decisions per side; control-delimited windows were tried and reverted — they made the canary side noisier). Abort on 2 consecutive gate-windows below −Y, or one below −1.5Y (collapse). Y = 3σ of the **live null**:

| pooled null (2 assignment seeds) | gate-windows | mean D | σ | min | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W = 1,000/side | 19 | 272.1 | 1,754.4 | −2,641.1 | 3,517.3 |
| W = 2,000/side | 9 | 167.8 | 1,399.3 | −2,384.5 | 2,518.0 |
| W = 4,000/side (gw) | 4 | 159.5 | 623.1 | −156.8 | 1,094.2 |

Y = 1,869 c/1k (routed) at the gate window; **zero breaches of Y or collapse on the null** [S3]. The bracketing of the √n law at W=4,000 on only 4 windows carries a ~35% error of its own — which is why Y is **re-measured continuously from live traffic** (at fleet pace a gate-window is 3.2 s; on the committed scenario's clock it is 2 h — the production gate re-measures the null as it goes, and a stage that has not yet formed a gate-window cannot abort on margin, only on routability/validity). Both sides face the same world in the same window, so world events difference out — verified against a real outage at [S4] (paired mean −68 c/1k over 4 gate-windows during a connection_refused event against Y=1,869; max |D| 2,082; zero false aborts; the Tier-1 transport alarms fired unsuppressed 349 s in).

**The gate is a set, and the excluded-processor row is why.** Excluding charlie — the ConstraintSet regression, the ticket's fear made concrete — produces D = −238.7 c/1k (inside margin resolution at 2 gate-windows) but +12.1 pts unroutable share and auth 61.3%: the candidate doesn't crash, it *silently strands* traffic, and its residual routing is only ~750 c/1k worse per routed decision. The margin branch alone would MISS it; the routability branch auto-aborts at seq 21,762 — **11,762 transactions at 25% share, ≈ 0.8 s of fleet wall clock after the window closes**. Conversely λ_to 45→0 (timeouts priced free) aborts on margin at seq 48,018 with routability silent. Two independent members, two different catches.

**Below-Y regressions fail to promote; they do not abort.** The ADR-0006 rejected sampler (normal-approximation draws) is the boundary case, measured: its margin D is *not worse* (+53.8 — starving exploration looks fine on a stationary world) and its coverage ratio sits inside the null band (0.92 vs 0.897–0.923) at a single 25% stage. It rides, the coverage check accumulates across stages, and the accepted residual risk is recorded: **a starvation-deploy can survive the 25% stage**; the ladder's later stages and #16's panels are what see it. λ_to 45→10 (−1,305 c/1k, below Y) is **promotion-BLOCKED** on the stage D-trace (mean < −2·s.e.) without aborting — the cap holds, the deploy does not ship. The same bad deploy gated vs not, over the same window [S3]:

| | margin ¢/1k (seq 10k–60k) | vs never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| naive 100% cutover, no gate | 22,495.8 | −4,892.5 |
| gated 25% with auto-abort | 27,062.9 | −325.4 |
| never deployed | 27,388.3 | — |

The gate prevents damage; it does not repair it. That asymmetry is the whole argument for gating before the money moves.

### 2. Warm-start strategy: fold inheritance, and what partial warm start is for

**The new policy inherits the old policy's *posterior*, not a summary of it.** The fold over the WAL is policy-independent learned state — per-arm world facts — so the candidate folds the same log from the incumbent's newest snapshot and is bit-identical in distribution to the incumbent's own state at boot [S1]: a warm no-op cutover (seed rotation only) is free (27,331.9 vs 27,443.0 c/1k full-run, indistinguishable; first-10k 27,724.9 vs 27,728.6). This is R47 and R102 applied to rollouts, and it is why shadow is not a learning wait.

**The cold case is priced and it is a defect.** A cutover that loses the store — same algorithm, same artifact, nothing wrong except missing state — pays **324 c/1k in its first 10k transactions** with MAE degrading 4.25 → 4.48 pts [S1]; 10k transactions is 1.2 days on the committed scenario's clock, 2 s at fleet pace. A cold shadow (agreement 59.9% rising to ~73%) is the same defect seen from the validation side. Neither is a stage to normalize: R102 makes a cold fold a rollout-blocking violation with a named owner (the migration), not a wait.

**Partial warm start from per-processor aggregate rates is valid — as a prior artifact, inside its own protocol.** The refresh path (2,000-transaction uniform probe → hierarchical rates at m=100, ADR-0006 §2) is a deployable `policy_id` and rides the same ladder. Measured on the improvement world [S5]: the refreshed artifact moves the direction (foxtrot share 15.3% vs 3.1% bare) but captures a fraction of the oracle headroom (a 2,000-probe artifact is itself a noisy estimate, and some of what it displaces was fine); a *miscalibrated* artifact (foxtrot ×1.25) rides the gate at D = −389.9 c/1k because at m=100 the fold's own data counts dominate a wrong prior within the cold window — bounded regret by ADR-0006's construction. The warm-start design **is** the prior-risk cap: the artifact can be wrong by 25% and the gate never fires, because the fold corrects it inside the ladder's own window.

### 3. Policy identity and version schema: hash is identity, semver is a label

`policy_id = sha256` over the canonical tuple: {algorithm, arm schema + band edges, prior artifact digest, policy seed, score/protocol config (λ_to, η, n_min, te_mode, draw_alg), catalog hash, ConstraintSet hash}. Measured [S0]: five distinct tuples → five distinct ids (`f644a705…` incumbent, `f9163503…` seed-rotation, `56d840d9…` λ 45→10, `be2b3e1e…` prior ×1.25, `6be97074…` draw_alg=normal); identical content rebuilds to the identical id (deploy time, instance, and shard are not identity inputs). This matches everything the repo already content-addresses (scenarios, catalogs, ConstraintSets, the audit chain) and it makes the rollout semantics exact: **a refreshed artifact, a λ retune, a sampler swap, or a seed rotation is a different policy and enters the ladder at shadow** — no exceptions, no "it's basically the same model".

Semver survives as the *human-facing label* on the same tuple (major = algorithm or arm-space change; minor = artifact/config refresh), because engineers ask "what changed between these two deploys" in words, not hex. It is never consulted by code: the DECISION_LOG v1 run-header hashes (ADR-0008) and the boot row's `config_hash`/`prior_hash`/`arm_schema_hash`/`catalog_hash` (ADR-0012 §6.3 — compare across shards rather than trusting config distribution) are the version hook. **Every decision row is trace-tagged with `policy_id`**, so #15's OPE separates versions by joining on the tag, and a transaction's side is re-derivable offline from `(rollout_seed, seq)` [R104] — the propensity set per version needs no new logging.

### 4. The automated rollback kill-switch: metric, window, trigger, speed

- **Metric**: the gate set of §1 — margin windows and routability abort; coverage, D-trace promotion, replay, and the ADR-0009 invariants block promotion.
- **Window**: matched equal-count W=1,000/side decision windows, gate-windows of gw=4 raw windows; abort state = 2 consecutive gate-windows below −Y, or 1 below −1.5Y, or 2 consecutive windows with unroutable-share delta > 5 pts.
- **Trigger**: automatic. No human approval step between the trigger condition and the swap — the trigger condition was calibrated on the null (0 breaches) and the regressions (2 aborts, both correct, 11,762 and 38,018 transactions after exposure at 25%), and a page accompanies it (the dashboard state flips to `rolled_back` with the offending window ids attached, #16).
- **Speed**: one config change — the atomic pointer swap of ADR-0012 to the previous `policy_id`'s share vector, sub-second propagation target. No fold rebuild (the incumbent fold never stopped), no lease stranding (ADR-0009's in-flight transactions complete under their own lease rows), no data migration. At fleet pace the total exposure of the charlie regression — from first gate-window to swapped — is seconds; on the committed scenario's clock it is hours, and the [S3] money column (gated −325.4 vs naive −4,892.5 c/1k vs never) is what the ladder bought. The propagation drill is #17's gate; the sandbox cannot measure a pointer swap it has no engine to swap.

## Payload for dependent tickets

- **#15 (OPE)**: the split is a pure function of `(rollout_seed, seq)` [R104] — re-derive it offline, do not parse logs for it. Rows carry `policy_id` [R101]; per-version propensity sets and cross-version contrasts join on that tag. Shadow rows exist in the log tagged as shadow: they are valid *validity* evidence and invalid *margin* evidence (plug-in grade only, ADR-0008 C4).
- **#16 (dashboard)**: the rollout panel shows, per stage: share, windows formed, D trace with **live Y and the null σ it came from**, per-member gate verdicts (margin / routability / coverage / promotion), suppression holds and refires (ADR-0007's ledger rows), and kill-switch state (`armed / rolled_back <policy_id> @ <seq>`). The Y display is load-bearing: a gate whose threshold is invisible invites someone to "tune" it per deploy.
- **#17 (benchmarks)**: three gates — (i) null false-abort rate = 0 over the committed steady scenario at the shipped window sizes (measured here 0/19 raw windows at 3σ(W=1), 0 gate-windows anywhere); (ii) the rollback drill: pointer-swap propagation time and lease-completion accounting on the real engine (this ADR's sub-second target is a labelled requirement, not a measurement); (iii) gate-window wall-clock at fleet pace (a W=1,000 window at 25% is 0.8 s at 5,000 dps — assert the pipeline keeps up).

## Consequences and design rules that follow

- **R100** — A rollout stage is a share vector over `policy_id`s. Promoting, demoting, or rolling back never edits a policy, an artifact, or a fold; it only moves the pointer. A deploy that needs a *different* candidate is a new rollout.
- **R101** — `policy_id` is the sha256 of the canonical tuple {algorithm, arm schema + band edges, prior artifact digest, policy seed, score/protocol config, catalog hash, ConstraintSet hash}. Equal content is the same policy; any component change is a new policy that enters the ladder at shadow. Semver is a label on the tuple, never an input to identity or to code.
- **R102** — One WAL, many folds. Every fold — incumbent, shadow, candidate — replays the same append-only settled-outcome log; a candidate bootstraps from the newest snapshot at or behind the WAL head. A cold fold (nothing to inherit) is an R102 violation and blocks the rollout; it is a migration defect, never a stage.
- **R103** — Shadow never executes: no dispatch, no lease, no ledger effect on the shadow side. Shadow decisions are logged and tagged; the only gates that read them are validity gates. `dbl = 0` and `I1 = 0` are computed on the executed side only.
- **R104** — Assignment is pure and key-addressed: side = f(rollout_seed, seq, purpose), i.i.d. per transaction, no ambient state (measured: 2,500 bp over 60,000 seqs = 25.08% [S0]). A re-decided transaction lands on the same side; #15 re-derives the split offline from the same inputs.
- **R105** — The gate is a set: margin windows + routability + coverage + replay PASS + `dbl=0`/`I1=0`, with the stage D-trace promotion check. All members must clear to promote; auto-rollback fires only on the margin and routability branches. Removing a member because "the margin gate caught everything last time" is how the charlie row ships.
- **R106** — Rollback is one config change: the atomic pointer swap to the previous `policy_id`'s share vector, sub-second propagation target, no fold rebuild. The incumbent fold never stopped folding; that is what makes rollback cheap and what makes R102 non-negotiable.
- **Costs accepted, named**: (i) the starvation residual — a single 25% stage cannot see ADR-0006's −2.2% arms-touched through TS's seed noise; accepted, checked cumulatively, and #16's panels own the later stages. (ii) The ramp premium on a *good* deploy — measured 240.8 c/1k over the [S5] deploy window (5.8 s at fleet pace) against an instant cutover of the same candidate; that is the insurance premium, and it is roughly free next to the naive row's −4,892.5. (iii) Suppression's delay when an improvement alarm lands inside the arming window — the re-check fires it (measured 4,485 transactions late at 256 settled; the arming window stays short by design), and the alternative (isolated canary detectors) starves by construction at 1–5% share. (iv) Shadow's +29% log volume while a rollout is in shadow — temporary by definition.
- **The gate never sleeps through a silent recovery**: [S4] shows the starved-improvement world's true Tier-2 alarm held once and refired at expiry (the shift was real), while steady-world share shifts trip nothing at δ=1e-3 — the ADR-0007 payload "share shifts must not false-trigger drift alarms" is closed with measurements on both sides.

## Reopen triggers

1. **The null fattens.** If σ at the gate window exceeds 2× the measured 623 c/1k on the committed scenario (Y > ~3,700 routed c/1k), the margin branch can no longer see the λ_to 45→10 class of regression (−1,305 c/1k) — window sizes must be re-derived, not the threshold lowered.
2. **A regression class walks the whole set.** Any deployable change with routed-D deficit > Y that no member catches (beyond the accepted starvation residual) reopens §1 — the set gets a member, not a louder margin gate.
3. **Fold inheritance stops buying what [S2] claims.** If cold-shadow agreement plateaus within 2 pts of the warm plateau on the committed scenarios, one-WAL-many-folds is not delivering its warm start and R102's cost/benefit is reopened.
4. **Suppression mis-sized.** If held-and-refired (true) alarms exceed half of all holds with mean delay > 4 h on the committed scenario clock, the arming window (256 settled) is wrong; if share shifts ever trip δ=1e-3 in steady state, [S4]'s "suppression is insurance, not a per-step cost" is wrong and every ramp step needs suppression pre-arming.
5. **The null goes heavy-tailed.** If live null gate-windows repeatedly breach 3σ in the *wrong* direction (min beyond −3σ more than ~1 in 50 windows), D is not symmetric enough for a σ-based Y and the gate moves to a quantile of the empirical null.

## Alternatives considered

- **Static Y from config (the ticket's literal "new underperforms old by >Y% for Z consecutive minutes").** Rejected: the null's σ is a property of the fleet, the window, and the seed (1,754 → 623 c/1k from W=1,000 to 4,000 here) — a constant is either always firing or never fires. The live-measured Y *is* the ticket's Y%·Z-minutes with the constants filled in by measurement, and the ADR keeps the re-measurement obligation so it cannot rot.
- **Sequential test on auth rate (SPRT), not margin.** Steelman: auth rate is (near-)binomial per attempt, so sequential bounds are textbook, and the charlie row's auth 61.3% would trip instantly. Rejected anyway: auth is not the estimand (ADR-0002) — the λ_to 45→0 row *improves* auth (74.7% vs 70.8%) while destroying margin (−2,451 c/1k) by pretending timeouts are free; an auth-based gate catches that one only after the money is gone. The margin gate is what the business bank account reads; the routability member catches the strandings that margin resolves too slowly.
- **Isolated canary streams/detectors (ADR-0007's literal "or isolate canary streams").** Rejected with numbers: a canary-only detector sees a quarter of the stream at 25% share (detection lag ×4 in fleet decisions) and starves outright at 1–5% — ADR-0007's own dilution failure. One blended stream per processor plus the suppression seam dominates; [S4] prices both sides.
- **100% cutover with instant rollback ("ship fast, revert fast").** The naive row is what it costs: −4,892.5 c/1k vs never over one window [S3], because rollback repairs *after* the money moved and the gate prevents *before*. Kept as the escalation path if the gate set is ever down (validity gates fail → do not promote, but do not roll back either).
- **Canary without shadow (fold straight to 1%).** Rejected: shadow is where replay bit-exactness, plumbing, and the constraint verdicts are validated at zero risk; [S2]'s cold-shadow curve shows the fold needs ~20k transactions to warm without inheritance — exactly the transactions a no-shadow rollout would spend learning on customers. Shadow costs 13 GB/day and one `Decide()`; it is the cheapest member of the ladder.
