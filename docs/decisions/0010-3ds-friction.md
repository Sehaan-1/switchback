# ADR-0010: 3DS friction — the authentication record, the fee the ledger charges, and MIT's scope

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-09-17
- **Resolves**: [#11 3DS friction and the reward function: modeling conversion loss from SCA challenges](https://github.com/Sehaan-1/switchback/issues/11)
- **Depends on**: [ADR-0002](0002-reward-function.md) (R9–R14: one learned quantity, closed taxonomy, T+1 computability, constraints-before-sampling, the eligibility log; the no-multiplier decision and Reopen Trigger 1), [ADR-0004](0004-constraint-layer.md) (R20/R22/R26: the eligible set is filtered before sampling and its census recorded; `regulatory.sca_required` is a pre-sampling fact), [ADR-0005](0005-simulation-harness.md) (the scenario grammar's `three_ds` block: frictionless rate, abandonment rate, liability-shift uplift, BIN/MCC multipliers), [ADR-0006](0006-thompson-sampling-implementation.md) (R39 arm key, R50 prior artifact, R49 floor), [ADR-0007](0007-drift-detection.md) (processor-level ADWIN + tiered decay: version attribution rides on it), [ADR-0008](0008-censored-exploration.md) (R60/R61 DECISION_LOG v1, R62 outcomes fold on arrival), [ADR-0009](0009-idempotency-double-charge.md) (R65: an abandoned challenge halts the chain)
- **Feeds**: #12 (engine: AUTH_RECORD emission, the fee model), #13 (state store: AUTH_RECORD layout and the join key), #15 (OPE: decomposition and exemption-claim provenance), #16 (dashboards: challenge/abandonment/unavailable panels, version attribution, exemption-budget burn), #17 (benchmarks: the fixture graduates; the harness scope predicate), #7 (arm granularity: no per-version arms), #5 (exemption class allow-list, liability budget, `auth_fee_minor`)
- **Evidence**: [`spikes/0011-sca-friction/RESULTS.md`](../../spikes/0011-sca-friction/RESULTS.md) — reproduce with `python3 spikes/0011-sca-friction/sca_friction.py 30000` (~14 s, stdlib only, deterministic; `sca-friction-v1@sha256:8089760b6cd8aadbf9e`, an overlay on `baseline-steady-v1@sha256:16661ded41dd`)

## Decision

1. **The reward does not change; the decomposition is an offline estimator.** ADR-0002 R9/R10 stand: θ = P(authorized | arm) is the only learned quantity, an abandoned challenge is a failure in the end-to-end label, and the ticket's `P(auth) × (1 − P(challenge)·P(abandon|challenge)) × margin` is the *decomposition of θ*, computed offline, never a multiplier applied to θ. ADR-0002's Reopen Trigger 1 is evaluated on the evidence and not met: abandonment loss is 4.8% of SCA margin fleet-wide, and the two processors that reach a 30% crossing inside the fixture's clamp need 38% and 66% abandonment to do it.
2. **AUTH_RECORD v1 — the authentication session is a first-class log record.** Every attempt that touches an authentication step emits a closed-vocabulary session record, joined to the decision by `seq`: `session_outcome` ∈ {`not_started`, `frictionless`, `challenged_completed`, `challenged_abandoned`, `authentication_failed`, `authentication_unavailable`, `attempted_processing`, `decoupled`, `exempt_accepted`, `exempt_refused`}, plus `flow_version`, `challenge_indicator`, exemption class/requested/accepted, `challenge_presented`, `challenge_completed`, `liability_shift`, and the fees actually incurred. The mapping rules are R72/R73: `U` (unavailable) is a technical failure and never a decline or an abandonment; `A` (attempted) is proof of attempt, not a completed challenge; `abandoned` means the challenge was presented and the customer left. The ticket's signal list is the subset ADR-0002 already named; this ADR makes it a record with denominators, because an estimate of `P(abandon | challenge)` needs sessions that reached a terminal state.
3. **The loss term charges the fee the ledger incurs** (extends ADR-0002 §1). A non-submitted failure — an abandoned or otherwise in-session terminal — is charged the authentication session fee, not the submission fee; the submission fee is charged only where there was a submission. Measured: the correction moves the loss term by +683 to +1,643 c/1k of attempts on this fleet and re-ranks nothing (0.00% of contexts change arm), so it is cost honesty before it is money — but a fee worth 5–78% of per-attempt margin cannot be missing from the score.
4. **The exemption claim is a constraint plus a signed liability budget, never a learned arm.** Fraud loss is outside the online reward (R11), so the online-optimal rule claims 100% of exemptions in every row of [S4] and the net position is negative from a 0.5× fraud-rate multiplier. The claim decision is therefore pre-sampling policy: an allow-listed class set (#5's ConstraintSet) and a liability budget the merchant signs; the claim and its class are logged (R72) so the budget is auditable and #15 can weight the action.
5. **`mandate=true` is scope, not a bucket.** An MIT whose mandate setup was authenticated is out of SCA scope (the EBA's MIT exclusion: a valid mandate, merchant-initiated, SCA at setup): the predicate is evaluated *before* the eligible set, so MIT traffic is exempt from the 3DS-capability filter (a non-3DS acquirer is legal for it) and from the challenge/dropout terms. A challenged MIT cannot be completed — there is no cardholder present — so the predicate has to be right, not learned (ADR-0009 R65: the chain halts). The `mandate` flag stays a dimension of the arm key (ADR-0006 R39). Measured: getting the predicate wrong costs 1,501 c/1k (5.4%) on the spike's 12% MIT mix, 2,719 c/1k under the fatal-challenge model, while the arm-key split itself is inside the fixture's noise (±0.1%).

## Context

Three facts change how the evidence should be read:

- **ADR-0002 already decided the scoring question, and this ticket was left the observation question.** #4 ruled the multiplier out with measured ablations (a 3DS multiplier is +64.8 ¢/1k on one synthetic fleet and −11.5 on another; censoring abandonment out of updates costs 2.2× posterior error for nothing). #11 was handed three jobs: estimate `P(challenge)` and `P(abandon | challenge)` for cold-start priors and OPE only, name the signals the engine must log, and decide how the mandate flag is treated. Every number below is about whether those estimates exist, what they are worth, and what must be true for them to be produced at all.
- **The funnel and the auth rate are different axes, and the fixture forces them apart.** In `sca-friction-v1` delta is the fleet's auth-strongest processor with a high-friction 3DS flow (39.0% challenged, 28.6% abandonment in [S0]) and charlie is auth-weak but frictionless (14.1%, 20.9%). A router that read only the authorization stream would rank foxtrot's 79.9% conditional over delta's 92.6% while foxtrot's end-to-end capture is 12 points below delta's. The two axes have to be estimated separately to be *attributed*; they must not be scored separately (that is the multiplier).
- **The committed harness does not carve MIT out of SCA scope.** `spikes/0006`'s `context()` derives `sca = region ∈ SCA_REGIONS and not exempt`; `mandate` is drawn from the mix and carried in the request but does not enter the scope predicate. Everything downstream of the eligible set inherits that. The spike models the predicate correctly in its own world and prices the gap in [S5]; closing it is a payload item for #17 (grammar) and #12 (engine), not something this ADR can fix from here.

## Consideration 1 — the outcome taxonomy

The ticket proposes four buckets: frictionless, challenge (with a 10–30% drop-off), exemptions (PSD2 low-value <€30, trusted beneficiary, MIT), and fallback non-3DS with merchant fraud liability. The ADR closes them into a session vocabulary (R72/R73) with three corrections:

- **"Frictionless" is not one outcome.** A session can be frictionless by issuer/ACS decision (challenge indicator 05, TRA performed, liability shifted or not) or frictionless by *us* claiming an exemption (acquirer-requested, liability with the merchant unless the issuer takes it). The two behave differently in the money: an exemption trades liability for conversion, exactly as [S4] prices, and a TRA can be accepted or overridden (the issuer's soft decline, scheme code RC65 or 1A, → retry with an SCA challenge at challenge indicator 04). Only a record that says *why* it was frictionless can support either the fraud budget or the liability-shift flag.
- **"Challenge" is a pipeline of terminal states, not a rate.** Presented → completed / failed / abandoned are separate states (a customer who left is not an issuer who refused), and the `U`/`A`/`decoupled` technical states sit beside them. In the fixture this matters at 2% (the session-unattributed share) and in the primary literature at ~2% of the 3DS2 flow; small, but it is the difference between an estimate and an interval.
- **The low-value rule is an exemption, and the grammar treats it as scope.** The fixture
  (and `spikes/0006`) model the <€30 amount rule and `sca_exemption_share` as scope, not as a
  claim: nothing is challenged either way, and no authentication means no liability shift in
  either case. The two are still different regulatory categories — an applied exemption
  consumes the issuer's counters and is reported as an exemption; an out-of-scope MIT is not
  — so when #17's grammar prices an exemption budget, the record (R72) must say whether a
  frictionless flow was *out of scope* or *exempted*, not merely that it was not challenged.
- **"Fallback non-3DS" is a liability class, not an availability fallback.** Routing SCA traffic to a non-3DS rail is not a valid fallback at all (the transaction is in scope; the acquirer cannot legally process it without an authentication), and the ADR forbids treating it as one: the eligible set is filtered by `sca_required` *and* capability (ADR-0004 R20). For MIT the same acquirer is legal and the liability question is different (see Consideration 5).

## Consideration 2 — processor selection

The ticket asks that selection "account for 3DS authentication success rate, not just authorization rate", and for per-version/per-scheme frictionless differences. The measured answer has three parts:

1. **Selection already accounts for authentication success, end-to-end.** θ = P(authorized | attempt) *is* the pipeline product: the abandoned challenge, the failed authentication and the technical failure are all in its denominator. What the decomposition adds is not a better ranking — [S2a] shows the composed prior does not beat the direct rate on a held-out mix — it is *attribution and transfer*: which part moved ([S2b]) and what a new arm's rate should be before it has its own data.
2. **Do not score the parts separately.** A separate challenge/abandonment term in the score is the multiplier ADR-0002 rejected, and [S3] shows why it stays rejected here: the loss is single-digit percent of margin on this fleet, and the arms where it is largest are the arms whose *margin* denominator is smallest, not the arms that are losing the merchant money.
3. **Version and scheme are logged dimensions, not arm dimensions** (R78). `flow_version` and the scheme are recorded, and a version-attributed regression rides ADR-0007's processor-level detector and #16's panels. Growing per-version arms multiplies the sparse-arm problem (#7's territory) and, on this fixture, a version change *is* a frictionless-rate change — which the record sees directly.

The fixture also exposes one approximation to name: `three_ds` multipliers are per merchant category, but the arm key (R39) does not carry MCC. An arm's prior therefore averages over the MCC mix. If #17's benchmarks show MCC-driven misranking of processors, the lever is arm granularity (#7), not a new term in the reward.

## Consideration 3 — the reward, and the data the estimate needs

The ticket's proposed formula is the decomposition of θ. ADR-0002's argument against applying it as a score is that the label already contains dropout; this spike adds the measurement that the *estimate* is the scarce thing, and prices what it takes:

- **Reopen Trigger 1, with numbers.** 4.8% fleet-wide on this fixture; the crossing rows exist but need 38%/66% abandonment on the thinnest-margin arms. The trigger stays live and now has a published value; if production shows an arm above 30%, the joint decomposition moves into the score under ADR-0002's own condition.
- **The data the estimate needs is a record, not a model.** `P(abandon | challenge)` is a ratio of two session terminal states. Without the terminal state the naive ratio is biased in the worst direction (+24.7 … +34.6 pts for the least-challenging arms) and the authorization stream cannot see the quantity at all ([S1]). The estimate is cheap once the record exists: at 2.5 standard errors, a 12-pt frictionless regression is visible in ~115 challenged sessions on the component and 708–6,089 attempts on capture ([S2b]).
- **The fee the score charges must be the fee the ledger shows** (R75). The fixture's [S6] finds the shipped loss term charging a submission fee on abandoned challenges that never submitted and missing the per-session authentication fee; the fix is a rule, not a tuning constant. R75 governs the *loss* term; whether `win` should also net the per-attempt submission fee on successful attempts (ADR-0002 §1 does not) is a neighbouring question the spike did not measure, and #5's fee model should answer it explicitly.

## Consideration 4 — data availability: abandonment is not imputed, it is logged

Abandonment is directly unobservable from the authorization stream — the customer simply never comes back — but it is observable *from the authentication session*, which is a system the merchant already runs (the 3DS server/ACS). The ADR's answer to "how do you impute it" is **don't**: R72 requires the session record; R73 requires the terminal states; a session whose outcome is not attributable (`session_unattributed`, the fixture's 1.1–2.2%) is counted separately and excluded from the rate, never redistributed by assumption. [S1c] is the argument made executable: two fleets with identical `P(challenge) × P(abandon|challenge)` and identical submission/capture rates are different fleets, and no imputation from the auth stream can tell them apart. Where the window is genuinely opaque — a processor that does not return session outcomes — the consequence is scoped: the decomposition is not estimable for that arm, and #15's reweighting must treat the arm's funnel as unobserved rather than invent one.

## Consideration 5 — mandate

`mandate=true` changes three things, and they all live in the same place:

1. **The scope predicate.** An authenticated-setup MIT is out of SCA scope (the EBA's MIT exclusion), so `sca_required` is false and the transaction is priced by the out-of-scope branch: no challenge, no dropout term, no liability-shift question. The measured cost of ignoring the flag is 1,501 c/1k (5.4%) on a 12% MIT mix, rising to 2,719 c/1k when the model admits that a challenged MIT cannot be completed ([S5]).
2. **The eligible set.** Scope is evaluated before the capability filter, so a non-3DS acquirer becomes legal for MIT (and only for it). On this catalog the widening never changes the oracle's choice (`to echo` 0.0% in every row) — echo is mid-pack on economics — but the rule is regulatory, and a benchmark where the widest-eligible arm *is* the cheapest belongs in #17 rather than being assumed away.
3. **The arm key.** The flag stays in the key (R39) so a policy can condition on it; the spike's pooled-vs-segmented check (30,844 vs 30,824 c/1k) says the key split is not where the money is on this fixture. The money is in the predicate. A policy that pools MIT with SCA traffic *while the predicate still treats MIT as in scope* is the failure mode this ADR is written to prevent: it averages a no-authentication population into a bucket whose reward terms describe authentication.

## Payload for dependent tickets

- **#12 (engine)**: emit AUTH_RECORD v1 per attempt at settlement; the fee model reads `auth_fee_minor` and charges by path (R75); the scope predicate (R77) is computed with the ConstraintSet, before the eligible set. No hot-path change beyond the record.
- **#13 (state store)**: AUTH_RECORD layout and retention, joined to DECISION_LOG v1 by `seq`; `session_unattributed` is a named counter; the record is not re-labelled on late settlement (ADR-0008 R62).
- **#15 (OPE)**: the decomposition fields are the offline estimand; an exemption claim is part of the action and changes the outcome distribution, so the claim (plan + class) is logged in the decision record, not only in the trace.
- **#16 (observability)**: panels for challenge rate, abandonment, unavailable share, by processor and `flow_version`; the composed-vs-direct prior check; exemption-budget burn against the signed budget.
- **#17 (benchmarks)**: the harness's `context()` must carve MIT out of scope (the §Context gap); `sca-friction-v1` graduates into the committed set with its hash pinned at that point; the benchmark needs a world where the non-3DS acquirer wins MIT traffic, and a 3DS-regression world at the abandonment rates [S2b] sweeps.
- **#7 (arm granularity)**: no per-version arms (R78); the R50 prior artifact may carry per-(proc × bin × region) components, but the direct end-to-end rate remains the default until a committed benchmark shows the composed prior winning on a held-out mix ([S2a]).
- **#5 (constraints)**: exemption class allow-list, the liability budget, and the `auth_fee_minor` catalog field.

## Consequences

**Easier.** The logging question is closed: one record, one vocabulary, one join key, and every rule above is checkable from it. `P(challenge)` and `P(abandon | challenge)` stop being hypothetical: they are ratios over terminal session states, cheap enough that a 12-pt regression is visible in ~115 challenged sessions. The mandate question stops being a modelling choice — it is a predicate over one flag, priced. The fee correction makes the reported margin reconcile with the ledger.

**Harder.** The engine grows a record it did not have (the 3DS server's session outcome is not the acquirer's response; #12 has to source it). The exemption decision becomes a signed budget someone owns, and a budget that is exceeded is a product conversation, not a code path. The scope predicate needs a fact the scenario grammar does not carry today: whether the mandate's setup was authenticated (our fixture's `mandate` flag cannot distinguish a SCA-authenticated mandate from an unauthenticated one — a named gap for #17's grammar).

### Design rules added

- **R72 — AUTH_RECORD v1.** Every attempt whose flow touched an authentication step emits `{seq, processor, attempt, flow_version, session_outcome (closed vocabulary), challenge_indicator, exemption_class, exemption_requested, exemption_accepted, challenge_presented, challenge_completed, liability_shift, session_fee_incurred, submission_fee_incurred}`, joined to DECISION_LOG v1 by `seq`. A session with no terminal state is `unattributed`: counted, excluded from rate denominators, never imputed.
- **R73 — Outcome mapping.** `authentication_unavailable` (`transStatus U`, a DS/ACS technical failure) is never a decline and never an abandonment; it is classified with transport failures for chain purposes. `attempted_processing` (`A`) is proof of attempt — `challenge_presented` true, `challenge_completed` false. `challenged_abandoned` is the only abandonment class; pre-session drop-off (`not_started`) and `unattributed` are their own classes.
- **R74 — No 3DS multiplier; the decomposition is offline.** θ stays end-to-end (R9/R10); `P(challenge)` and `P(abandon | challenge)` are estimated from AUTH_RECORD for R50 priors and #15 reweighting only. A composed prior ships only if a committed benchmark beats the direct rate on a held-out mix; on this fixture it does not.
- **R75 — Fee incidence.** The loss term charges the fee the ledger incurred: submission fee iff a submission happened; authentication session fee iff a session ran. The reported margin must reconcile with the ledger.
- **R76 — Exemption discipline.** Exemption claims are pre-sampling policy over an allow-listed class set plus a merchant-signed liability budget — never a learned arm, and never a post-sampling veto. The claim and its class are logged (R72) and the budget's burn is #16's panel.
- **R77 — Mandate scope.** `sca_required` is false for an MIT whose mandate setup was authenticated; the predicate is evaluated before the eligible set, so such traffic is exempt from the 3DS-capability filter and from challenge/dropout terms. A challenged MIT is never retried to another rail (ADR-0009 R65). The `mandate` flag remains a dimension of the arm key (R39).
- **R78 — Version and scheme are logged dimensions, not arm dimensions.** `flow_version` and scheme are recorded (R72) and used for attribution; the router does not grow per-version arms.

## Reopen triggers

1. **ADR-0002's Trigger 1, production numbers.** The measured floor on this fixture is 4.8% fleet-wide (per-arm maximum 19.0%); if any production arm sustains >30% abandonment loss as a share of expected margin, the joint decomposition moves into the score and this ADR is superseded on that point.
2. **T+1 fraud data** (ADR-0002 Trigger 2). If expected fraud loss becomes computable inside the update window, the exemption claim can re-enter the objective and R76 becomes a preference rather than a constraint.
3. **The `unattributed` share rises above ~5% of sessions on any processor** — the record is not being produced; the funnel estimate is an interval again and that processor's decomposition should be withdrawn rather than imputed.
4. **A 3DS version migration moves a processor's frictionless rate by more than 5 pts** — [S2b]'s panel must attribute it; if version is the cause, consider per-version priors (still not arms).
5. **A merchant whose MIT share exceeds 50% and whose cheapest eligible rail is non-3DS** — re-run [S5]'s eligible-set half on that catalog; this fixture cannot price it.

## Alternatives considered

- **The ticket's multiplier as the score** (`P(auth) × (1 − P(dropout)) × margin`): rejected — ADR-0002's double count, and [S3] shows the loss term this fixture would add is single-digit percent of margin, concentrated on the arms with the thinnest margin denominator. The formula survives as the offline decomposition, which is what the ticket actually asks to estimate.
- **Imputing abandonment from the authorization stream** (the naive subtraction, or a model of "non-submitted = abandoned"): rejected on [S1] — it is biased by +24.7 … +34.6 pts precisely against the arms with the least friction, inverting the ranking, and [S1c] shows the quantity is not identifiable from the auth stream at all. The honest output without a session log is an interval.
- **A separate learned challenge/abandonment posterior in the score**: rejected for the same reason as the multiplier — the session outcome is available at T+1, so computability is not the blocker; the double count is. The decomposition is a read-only estimate.
- **Exemption as a learned arm**: rejected — [S4] measures the online rule claiming 100% of exemptions and the net position negative from 0.5× fraud; the reward cannot price what the arm gives up, so the decision belongs in the constraint/budget layer (R76).
- **Per-processor 3DS-flow and non-3DS-flow arms** (ADR-0002's deferred alternative): still deferred to #7 rather than rejected. The record this ADR requires is what would make such arms estimable, but the sparse-arm cost and the exemption-logic dependency are unchanged.
- **Pooling MIT into the non-SCA bucket and dropping the flag from the key**: measured, not argued — 30,844 vs 30,824 c/1k on [S5], inside the fixture's noise. Retaining the flag costs a bucket and keeps the policy space honest; the failure this ADR is written against is scope, not key cardinality.
- **Treating the mandate flag as sufficient for scope**: rejected — scope requires that the mandate's *setup* was authenticated; the fixture's flag cannot express that, and the grammar gap is named rather than assumed away.
